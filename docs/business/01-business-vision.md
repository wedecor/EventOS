## Executive Summary

We Decor Events is a Bangalore-based event decoration and experience company founded approximately four years ago by **Ilyas** and **Zakir**. The company serves individuals and families who want professional, customized decorations for life celebrations—especially **home events** such as birthdays, Haldi, Mehndi, engagements, baby showers, anniversaries, and Nikah ceremonies.

We Decor does not sell fixed packages. Every quotation is customized. Revenue comes almost entirely from decoration services. The company competes on **quality, customization, reliability, and budget-friendly pricing**—not on being the cheapest option. It holds **75+ five-star Google reviews** and generates enquiries primarily through its **website, Instagram, Google Ads, SEO, referrals, and repeat customers**.

The business is at an inflection point: marketing generates strong enquiry volume (~**200/month** when ads are active, ~**10% conversion**), but **operational coordination**—not lead generation—is the binding constraint. A team of **two founders, two permanent decorators, and a trusted temporary decorator pool** currently handles up to **25 events per month** in peak periods, with a 12-month target of **30+ events/month** at consistent quality.

We Decor is deliberately building a brand around **home celebrations** while still accepting venue and larger events. Long-term, the founders intend to evolve from decoration into a **full-fledged event management company**, powered by **technology and operational excellence** as a sustainable competitive advantage.

## Why We Decor Exists

We Decor Events exists to make celebrating life's special moments beautiful, memorable, and stress-free—without requiring customers to spend months planning every detail.

The founders observed that many people wanted professionally decorated weddings, birthdays, engagements, baby showers, Haldi ceremonies, Mehndi functions, and other celebrations, but the process of finding reliable decorators, understanding pricing, comparing ideas, and coordinating execution was confusing and time-consuming.

We Decor's goal is to simplify that journey. The company helps customers transform their vision into reality by providing creative decoration solutions, transparent quotations, reliable execution, and a seamless experience from the first enquiry to the completion of the event.

We Decor is not positioned as a decoration vendor alone. It is an **event experience company** that combines creativity, operational excellence, and customer service to create memorable celebrations—while giving customers confidence that everything will be handled professionally.

As the business grows, the founders intend for technology and automation to remove operational complexity so the team can spend more time serving customers and creating exceptional events, rather than managing spreadsheets, phone calls, and repetitive administrative work.

## Core Belief

> **Every celebration is unique, but running an event business should not be chaotic.** Technology should automate operations while allowing the team to focus on creativity, relationships, and delivering exceptional experiences.

A related principle that governs day-to-day decisions:

> **Customer trust is more valuable than short-term profit.** Every event is an opportunity to build reputation. The company is willing to sacrifice profit on a project if it means keeping the promises made to the customer.

## Company Values

These values are derived directly from founder interviews—not aspirational branding. They explain how We Decor operates and how decisions are made when trade-offs arise.

| Value | What It Means at We Decor |
|-------|---------------------------|
| **Customer Trust Before Profit** | Honor agreed quotations even when material costs rise unexpectedly; absorb losses rather than renegotiate after booking confirmation |
| **Every Event Matters** | Treat a ₹3,000 birthday and a large wedding with equal care—every event represents a special moment in a customer's life |
| **Deliver What We Promise** | If a design or decoration is committed to, execute it as promised; reputation is built event by event |
| **Quality Without Compromise** | One poorly executed event can damage years of trust; quality of execution is non-negotiable |
| **Continuous Improvement** | Operational problems are solved through better processes and technology—not accepted as inevitable chaos |

| **Professionalism** | Reliable communication, on-time setup, skilled execution, and respectful treatment of customers |

### How Values Translate to Decisions

When faced with a difficult choice, We Decor's decision logic is:

1. **Can we deliver this event well?** If no → decline the booking.
2. **Have we committed to a price and design?** If yes → honor it, even at reduced margin.
3. **Will this protect or damage customer trust?** Trust wins over short-term profit.
4. **Can we solve this operationally?** Improve planning, systems, or coordination—don't lower standards.

## Business Operating Principles

Company Values explain **what We Decor believes**. Business Operating Principles explain **how the founders make decisions** when running the business day to day.

Each principle below is derived from founder interviews. These are operational decision rules—not marketing statements.

### Speed Matters

**Description:** In a competitive market, customers contact multiple decorators before deciding. The speed of response and consistency of follow-up directly affect whether an enquiry converts into a booking.

**Real-world implication:** Delayed response or missed follow-up often means the customer books elsewhere. Enquiry handling is time-sensitive, not administrative backlog.

**Example from We Decor:** The founders identified that most customers contact multiple decorators through Instagram, Google, and other platforms. If We Decor does not respond quickly or follow up on time, there is a high chance the customer will book with another decorator.

---

### Customer Trust Compounds Over Time

**Description:** Every event is an opportunity to build or damage reputation. Trust earned through consistent delivery generates referrals, repeat customers, and reviews that reduce dependence on paid acquisition.

**Real-world implication:** Short-term margin decisions are evaluated against long-term reputation impact. One poorly executed event can outweigh years of positive reviews.

**Example from We Decor:** We Decor holds 75+ five-star Google reviews. Many enquiries come through referrals, repeat customers, Instagram, and online presence built on accumulated trust—not a single transaction.

---

### Own Customer Relationships—Do Not Rely on Aggregators

**Description:** Sustainable growth comes from owned brand, channels, and customer relationships—not third-party platforms that control access to customers.

**Real-world implication:** Invest in website, SEO, Google Ads, Instagram, and referral relationships. Treat aggregator platforms as experiments, not strategy.

**Example from We Decor:** The first customer came through JustDial, but JustDial was not a sustainable growth channel. The turning point came when the founders launched their own website, focused on SEO, and ran Google Ads and Instagram marketing—which became primary sources of enquiries and growth.

---

### Technology Should Remove Repetitive Work

**Description:** Founders and staff time should be spent on creativity, customer relationships, and event delivery—not on repetitive administrative coordination that systems can handle.

**Real-world implication:** When operational pain recurs, the default response is to solve it through process and technology—not to accept it as normal industry chaos.

**Example from We Decor:** As enquiries grew, WhatsApp made it difficult to track leads, follow-ups, quotations, and conversions. Ilyas built the Lead Management Application and Quotation and Billing Application rather than scaling manual coordination indefinitely.

---

### Every Operational Challenge Is an Opportunity to Improve

**Description:** Problems in lead management, event coordination, or execution are signals to improve processes—not permanent conditions of running an event business.

**Real-world implication:** Operational failures (such as a late arrival) trigger strengthened planning and systems—not acceptance as unavoidable.

**Example from We Decor:** After a team arrived approximately 30 minutes late due to operational challenges, the founders treated it as a learning experience and strengthened planning—because timing matters to customers.

---

### Know Which Channels Drive Growth

**Description:** Marketing investment and growth strategy depend on understanding which channels produce enquiries and bookings—not guessing or relying on platforms that cannot scale.

**Real-world implication:** Lead source tracking and marketing attribution are operational requirements, not optional analytics. Channels that cannot be sustained should be replaced with owned alternatives.

**Example from We Decor:** JustDial delivered the first customer but proved unsustainable. Today, lead sources are tracked manually by asking customers how they found We Decor or identifying which platform they contacted—there is currently no automated attribution system.

---

### Honor Commitments—Even When It Costs Margin

**Description:** Once a quotation is agreed and a booking is confirmed, the price and scope stand. Unexpected cost increases are absorbed rather than passed to the customer.

**Real-world implication:** Pricing decisions at quotation time must account for risk. Post-booking price increases are not used to recover margin.

**Example from We Decor:** On occasions when market prices for flowers or materials increased after booking confirmation, We Decor absorbed the additional cost and completed the event at lower profit or at a loss rather than asking the customer to pay more.

---

### Decline Rather Than Deliver Poorly

**Description:** If We Decor cannot execute an event to the standard it commits to, the booking should not be accepted.

**Real-world implication:** Capacity, scheduling, and capability are evaluated honestly before confirmation—not after the customer has paid an advance.

**Example from We Decor:** The founders stated they would rather decline an event than accept it and fail to meet customer expectations. Deals are occasionally lost when dates are unavailable due to existing bookings—rather than overcommitting.

---

### Every Event Deserves Equal Care

**Description:** Event size and budget do not determine the level of effort or care. A ₹3,000 birthday and a large wedding both represent special moments in a customer's life.

**Real-world implication:** Resource allocation and attention are not tiered by ticket size. Marketing may focus on home celebrations, but execution standards do not vary by budget.

**Example from We Decor:** The founders explicitly treat every event equally regardless of size or budget, and state that every event is important because it represents a special moment in the customer's life.

## Founder Philosophy

This section summarizes the mindset behind We Decor—not a mission statement, but how the founders think about building the business.

### Why Technology Matters

Ilyas comes from a software engineering background. When We Decor faced operational pressure—leads lost in WhatsApp, follow-ups missed, quotations disconnected from enquiries—his instinct was to solve problems with technology, not to hire more coordinators or accept chaos as normal.

Technology is not the product We Decor sells to customers. It is how the founders intend to remove operational complexity so the team can focus on creativity, relationships, and delivering events. The goal is for staff to spend time serving customers and creating exceptional events—not managing spreadsheets, phone calls, and repetitive administrative work.

### Why Customer Trust Matters

Customer trust is more valuable than short-term profit. We Decor's reputation is built event by event. The founders believe one poorly executed event can damage years of trust—while consistent delivery creates referrals, repeat business, and reviews that sustain growth without relying on aggregators.

Trust is won when the customer sees the completed decoration for the first time. It is confirmed when they recommend We Decor to friends and family or leave a positive Google review.

### Why Every Event Matters

We Decor does not treat small events as practice for large ones. Whether it is a simple birthday decoration at home or a large wedding celebration, every event represents a special moment in someone's life. The founders treat them with equal care because the customer's emotional stake—not the invoice amount—defines what is at risk.

### Why Quality Matters More Than Short-Term Profit

The founders have absorbed material cost increases after booking confirmation rather than renegotiate with customers. They have completed events at reduced profit or at a loss to honor commitments. This is deliberate: reputation compounds; margin on a single event does not.

Quality of execution is non-negotiable. Delivering what was promised matters more than optimizing every rupee on every project.

### Why Relationships Matter More Than Transactions

We Decor is building an event experience company—not a transaction business that optimizes for volume. Zakir communicates directly with customers, shares designs, and builds understanding of their vision. The hassle-free promise means that after booking, the customer should not worry about decoration.

The founders want customers to describe We Decor as reliable, professional, easy to work with, and caring—not merely affordable. Referrals and repeat customers are evidence that relationships, not single transactions, drive the business.

### Why Owned Brand Matters

JustDial delivered the first customer but not a scalable business. The founders learned that depending on platforms they do not control is fragile. Building website, SEO, Instagram, and Google presence is building an asset We Decor owns—customer relationships that do not disappear when a platform changes.

The home celebrations focus is a **deliberate strategy developed over the last couple of years**. Initially, We Decor accepted every type of event regardless of size. ## The Customer Experience

### What "Hassle-Free" Means

Once a customer shares their requirements and confirms a booking, We Decor takes responsibility for turning their vision into reality with **as little effort from the customer as possible**. The customer should not worry about decoration after booking—the team handles planning, setup, execution, and timely completion.

### Typical Customer Journey

```
1. Customer contacts via WhatsApp, Instagram, Google, or website
2. Ilyas collects basic details and creates enquiry in Lead Management Application
3. Enquiry assigned to Zakir for direct customer communication
4. Zakir understands requirements, budget, event details, venue; shares designs and ideas
5. Customer finalizes design and quotation; advance payment collected to confirm booking
6. Team plans event, arranges materials, prepares for execution
7. On event day: decorators arrive, complete setup on time, ready before celebration begins
8. Remaining payment collected after event completion
9. With permission: photos and videos captured for Instagram and portfolio
```

### Division of Responsibility (Founder Roles in Customer Journey)

| Stage | Primary Owner |
|-------|---------------|
| Enquiry capture and system entry | Ilyas |
| Customer communication, design, quotation | Zakir |
| Event planning, materials, execution | Zakir and operations team |
| Marketing and lead generation | Ilyas |

### Customer Concerns Before the Event

Even with strong communication, customers may worry about:

- Whether decoration will look exactly as promised
- Whether everything will be completed on time

Building trust during communication and delivering exactly what was committed to are central to the business.

### The "Trust Won" Moment

Trust is confirmed when the customer sees the completed decoration for the first time—their reaction, appreciation, and positive feedback. Referrals to friends and family and Google reviews are secondary confirmation of success.

An event is successful not only when decoration looks beautiful, but when the customer can enjoy their celebration without worrying about the decoration process.

### How Customers Should Describe We Decor

If recommending to a friend, the founders want customers to say:

> *"They are reliable, professional, easy to work with, deliver what they promise, provide beautiful customized decorations at reasonable prices, and genuinely care about making every celebration special."*

## What We Will Never Become

Based on founder principles and stated decisions—not marketing positioning.

| We Decor Will Never… | Why |
|----------------------|-----|
| **Compete primarily on being the cheapest** | Lost deals come from price shoppers, but We Decor competes on quality, customization, and reliability at budget-friendly—not lowest—prices |
| **Sacrifice quality or trust for growth** | One poor event can damage years of reputation; growth that degrades quality is unacceptable |
| **Accept bookings that cannot be executed well** | Founders would rather decline than accept and fail customer expectations |
| **Increase price after booking confirmation** | Material cost increases have been absorbed rather than passed to customers |
| **Use hidden charges** | Transparency is a core value; additional costs only for customer-requested scope changes |
| **Force customers into predefined packages** | Every event is customized; starting prices are entry points, not fixed bundles |
| **Treat smaller events as less important** | Equal care for ₹3,000 birthdays and large weddings |
| **Depend on aggregator platforms for growth** | JustDial was a starting point, not a strategy; owned channels are primary |
| **Become a rental or commission-based business** | Revenue is from decoration services only—no rental business, no vendor markups |
| **Lose the personal, trust-based customer experience** | Hassle-free means customers should not worry after booking; trust is won through delivery |

| Home celebrations are an underserved growth segment | Deliberate focus without excluding other event types |
